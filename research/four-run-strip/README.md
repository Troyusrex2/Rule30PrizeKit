# Four equal readings recur in every strip of 19 singleton columns

Fix any 19 consecutive columns of the Rule 30 spacetime from one black cell. At least one fixed column in that strip has four consecutive readings of one color at arbitrarily late times. The color can also be fixed by pigeonhole. The column and color can depend on the strip. Applied to columns −11 through +7 this puts an infinitely recurrent event within nine sites of −2. The proof gives no preference to the middle of the strip.

## Proof

Suppose that from time U onward all 19 columns avoid both 0000 and 1111. Make a directed graph whose vertices record three successive rows of a fixed-width strip. An edge records four rows. They overlap their neighbor in three rows. Each cell's four-bit word must be nonconstant. For each tick the interior cells obey Rule 30. At each strip end allow any exterior bit compatible with that update. Every true orbit gives a walk under these rules.

An infinite walk in a finite graph eventually uses only edges within a strongly connected component. Its projection on each narrower substrip also eventually has this property. This makes the following pruning exact for recurrent edges. Start with width two. To extend a surviving four-row window one column east, try all 14 nonconstant four-bit words. Check the update at the previous eastern end using the new column, and check that some exterior word permits the new eastern end. Then discard edges between different strongly connected components. A cycle at the larger width projects to a closed walk at the smaller width, so none of its edges can be lost. Conversely every retained edge lies on a cycle of locally legal strip windows. Only the forward implication is needed for the singleton.

At width 19 this leaves **495 edges**. Project their source and destination to the **westernmost 12 columns**. This gives **217 three-row states**, each with exactly one next state and exactly one previous state. The cycle lengths, with multiplicity, are 3 (four), 4 (seven), 5 (one), 8 (one), 14 (one), 19 (two), 37 (two) and 38 (one). Consequently every infinite avoiding walk eventually has periodic readings at each of these twelve columns, with a common period at most 38. Even two such columns contradict Jen's theorem for the singleton. Finitely many positions and colors yield the fixed-position, fixed-color conclusion above. [Jen's theorem in the kit](https://github.com/WolframInstitute/Rule30PrizeKit/blob/bd5ec59a/lean/Ruliology/CellularAutomata/Rule30Jen.lean)

The computer-assisted step is a finite graph claim. Run `python verify.py` with Python 3 and its standard library. A second construction uses pairs of overlapping recurrent strips and NetworkX's strong-component algorithm. Run `python compare.py` with NetworkX available to compare the two sets of edges element by element. The 19-column conclusion has a paper proof using these replays. Its graph certificate has not been replayed in Lean.

## Why the location matters

The kit supplies a finite right half which, under an imposed 001 clock, stays inside its period-15 trap forever. Its masked right-neighbor trace is 18864 in the kit's encoding. This determines the entire west half of a full-line orbit by solving the update for the unknown western neighbor at each depth. The actual right-neighbor bits at black clock phases can be discarded in this calculation because the OR already equals one. [Trap and finite right origin](https://github.com/WolframInstitute/Rule30PrizeKit/blob/bd5ec59a/lean/Ruliology/CellularAutomata/Rule30EtherThreeNotAbsorption.lean)

For a period-15 column c and its eastern neighbor r, the next column west is the one-tick advance of c XOR (c OR r). Iterating the ordered pair gives a spatial transient of 83 and cycle of 2,145. The longest run of western columns avoiding temporal four-runs has length six, including across the cycle seam. Thus **every seven consecutive columns at or west of the center contain an infinitely recurrent four-run, while column −2 never has one**. Run `python control.py`. This orbit starts with infinitely many black cells to the west. If it had a leftmost black cell, the two periodic adjacent western columns would contradict the finite-left form of Jen's argument. The example rules out transporting the band observation to −2 using only this clock and a finite right start.

There is a stronger localization control if one also wants to keep Jen's conclusion. Fix the literal singleton initial row at and east of −2. Prescribe its genuine trace at −2 through any chosen finite time, followed by an arbitrary infinite concatenation of 101 and 110. This tail has no four-run. For each new prescribed time t, exactly one choice of the initial bit at −2−t gives that reading. Flipping the bit flips the rightmost tip of its light cone, since the left input enters the rule by XOR. Earlier readings are unaffected. This constructs uncountably many full-line initial rows with the specified eastern half and genuine prefix.

For any observation site j, fixing its entire initial eastern half makes its full temporal trace injective as a function of its initial western half. Two differing rows have a rightmost difference, whose right-moving tip reaches j. If j is west of −2 there are only finitely many choices for the additional initial bits through −2. Thus at each j only countably many of the constructed rows have an eventually periodic trace there. Discard these for all j and also discard the countably many rows with finite western support. The remaining full histories have **no eventually periodic column anywhere**, so the 19-column proof applies to all of them, yet −2 avoids four-runs forever after its genuine prefix. This argument identifies exactly which singleton information those hypotheses lose. It gives no eventual period at the center of these control histories.

## A second fixed-site route to excluding period three

Under either eventual period-three center clock, the five-letter word **10101** eventually disappears from column −6. Thus proving it recurs there arbitrarily late would exclude both clocks. The Lean proof reconstructs five columns west of column −1 from a 12-tick window on −1 and the center. At most four free bits enter, and all 16 choices are allowed in the alternating-word proof. The singleton implication uses the kit's established phase skeleton and normalization of period three.

With the kit's short exclusions on the free bits, the local table also tells the colors of four-runs. Under 001, western free bits 0011 produce two overlapping four-white windows starting at phases 1 and 2, making five white readings. Under 011, western free bits 0010 produce a four-black window starting at phase 0. These are all possible starts in an aligned triple under the respective restrictions. Kernel replay covers the physical window table, the eventual alternating-word exclusion, and the implication from fixed-site recurrence to exclusion of period three. Neither cofinal recurrence at −6 nor cofinal four-runs at −2 has been proved.

We tried to prove the disjunction directly. Require column −6 to avoid 10101 and column −2 to avoid both monochrome four-letter words. In the five-column strip with these endpoints, the four-row state (0, 0, 0, 16) admits two return walks. In decimal row masks, western bit first at bit zero, their appended rows are A = (24, 13, 4, 31, 0, 0, 0, 16) and B = (24, 12, 22, 18, 31, 0, 0, 0, 16). AB and BA each return in 17 ticks and have distinguishable outputs at **each** of the five sites. Concatenating them according to an aperiodic block sequence makes every strip column aperiodic: at each site one fixed offset decodes the block choice. This defeats the Jen contradiction using only this strip and these two endpoint conditions. The walk has freely chosen exterior bits; full-orbit realization with the singleton's exterior remains open. Run `python two_sites.py` to reconstruct and check the witness.

On paper the kit's gate timing also bounds the gaps between these markers once the gate has settled. For 001 the start-to-start gap is at most 27 ticks, since a complete gate run of each color occupies at most nine gate samples together. For 011 a free left one is a white right-gate sample. Once there is one late enough, the next lies at most 17 gate samples ahead, hence at most 51 ticks. There must be arbitrarily late such samples under the singleton hypothesis, since an eventually constant free stream would make column −1 periodic beside the periodic center. These timing consequences use the existing trap-entry and gate-gap theorems in addition to the new local table.
