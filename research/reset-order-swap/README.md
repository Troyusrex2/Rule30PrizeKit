# Swapping two reset gaps at the fixed white wall

Consider Rule 30 on positions −1, −2, … with position 0 held white. A reset here means a zero at position −1, with ones until the next reset. Suppose the first two gaps are 6 and 10, in either order, and the next 24 gaps are each 6 or 10. For **every** initial row with that prefix, the two gaps can be interchanged by changing only cells of kit depth less than 174. The two traces differ exactly at ticks 6 and 10. The entire physical rows coincide from tick 11 onward. Any finite starting row can be replaced by one of the same width. The future after those 24 gaps is unrestricted.

The zero-boundary reconstruction fixes the whole spacetime from the near-wall trace. Once the two edited traces agree from tick 11, so do all their reconstructed columns from that tick on. For each common prefix of future gap choices, pack a temporal column into an integer, with the earliest time in the low bit. A spatial step sends (a,b) to (b,(b shifted right one bit) XOR (a OR b)). At a leaf the low 11 bits of **both** columns agree between the two reconstructions. The test uses only bits prescribed by the prefix. All later spatial columns must then agree for every common future. The binary tree has 1,272 leaves covering every choice of the 24 further gaps. The deepest synchronization occurs at kit depth 174. The longest prefix actually read by a leaf has 193 ticks.

Finite width follows from the outermost black cell moving one cell outward per tick. Two finite starting rows that meet must have equal widths. For an example obtained by taking the 24 extra gaps all equal to six, the initial integers are 1227821142100895741738805486657763235399975977238 and 1227821142100895741738805486657763235468979175766. Bit zero is the cell at position −1.

## What changes at a later reset

If a finite starting row has width W, its first W near-wall observations determine all its potentially nonzero cells. Therefore any different trace which preserves these W observations and eventually reaches the same physical row must have an infinite starting row. In the setting above, a swap performed at a reset at time s≥W starts from a finite alternative at time s. Restoring the original wall observations before s forces an earlier row which is black at every sufficiently large depth, immediately followed by a finite row. Beyond a finite image, the two-register backward rule is Boolean OR, which proves the black-tail assertion. These statements are formalized as [late_edit_infinite and late_swap_transition](https://github.com/Troyusrex2/Rule30PrizeKit/blob/0fc65a8779365c3d8a0b8f8764ec866cc1e057d5/lean/Ruliology/CellularAutomata/Rule30ResetSwap.lean).

A concrete finite-history example shows how this obstruction appears. Start with 2440786724530854946624415088918 (width 101). Its near-wall gaps are legal through tick 116. Swapping the returns starting at tick 100 changes just observations 106 and 110, and the rows coincide at tick 111. At tick 72 the alternative is still finite, but **neither** of the two possible near-wall bits at tick 71 has a finite predecessor for that tick-72 row. This example checks a specific finite history independently of the 24-gap hypothesis. It explains why repeatedly sorting returns at arbitrary positions would need another theorem.

The infinite period-two exclusion remains open. A hypothetical finite origin with an infinite mixed schedule would give swaps arbitrarily late and therefore the black-tail transition above. What is missing is a reason this transition cannot coexist with an indefinitely legal continuation in the finite original orbit. A legal finite prefix alone allows the obstruction.
