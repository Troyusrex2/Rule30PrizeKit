What is STILL missing
The theorem does not show that every relevant legal reconstructed row has
recurrent resets. It assumes this. It also does not connect a possibly false
general legal-row conjecture to all periods without the precise prize bridge.
The global infinite-ancestry obstruction remains open. No prize is claimed.

The global count law is sufficient, not the only possible remaining claim
Swarm proposes N(d,L)<=C*A^d*lambda^L with one C,A and0<lambda<1 valid for
all d,L. Since N is an integer, such an ABSOLUTE count bound would force
extinction at each fixed d; density/probability decay alone would not.
But the proposed uniform law is substantially stronger than merely finite
extinction for each d. It implies a LINEAR upper bound in d on every live
depth L, by1<=C*A^d*lambda^L and taking logarithms.

A simple logical counterfamily shows the distinction: let the d-th tree be
one branch of length d^2. Every tree has finite height (so every infinite
input is killed), but no fixed C,A,lambda of the displayed form bounds its
live counts for all d,L: at L=d^2 the left side is1 and the right side tends
to0. This is a generic logical example, NOT a Rule30 counterexample.
Thus calling that entropy law "exactly the one open statement" is too strong.
A deterministic, nonuniform well-founded argument could suffice instead.
The observed finite slope/decay is a conjecture generator, not its proof.
