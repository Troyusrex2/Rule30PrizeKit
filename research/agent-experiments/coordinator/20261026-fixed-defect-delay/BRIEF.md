# The sink needs a bound or invariant that sees the shared background

The [new full kernel proof](REPORT.md) shows why a nearby-source argument
alone cannot finish the reader. For every T there are finite starts with the
same initial four-cell defect at positions 1..4, source 4 opposite forever,
shared nonconstant neighbor 5, and initial reader state 15, whose observed
cells agree through T. Their shared background has width 2*T+8.

This is not a sink-completeness counterexample. It rules out a uniform
deadline depending only on defect size/distance. Bounds depending on total
background width, recursive temporal witnesses and global boundary arguments
remain possible. Every one of the 13 tested truncations eventually separates;
that sample is not a proof for all truncations, much less the whole sink.

The finite examples come from truncating a shared periodic infinite tail.
The untruncated pair resynchronizes forever, even with finite initial
difference and a permanently opposite source. Thus a local lemma valid
without the white-tail condition is too strong. An earlier eight-clock
period-loss orbit is a precursor; do not claim that phenomenon as new.
