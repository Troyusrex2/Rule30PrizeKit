# What the singleton's western origin forces

## The old five-column counterexample

In the strip with west endpoint −6 and east endpoint −2, call the two earlier return walks A and B. The equal-length coding used AB and BA. Two consecutive B tiles would give the nine row masks 18, 31, 0, 0, 0, 16, 24, 12, 22, with the western bit at position zero. Evolve the eight cells immediately east of the strip under these easternmost readings. None of the 256 initial patterns can reproduce the last column given its neighbor inside the strip. Thus a full orbit cannot change from AB to BA. It can change from BA to AB at most once. Any infinite coded path would eventually repeat every 17 ticks on two adjacent columns, contradicting Jen for the singleton. This rules out the original equal-length coding. It leaves other walks, including other choices among the raw A and B tiles, to be examined.

There is also a finite bound that uses the actual initial row. Suppose an aligned run of identical 17-tick blocks lasts L ticks beginning at age T, including its initial and final row. The two western columns agree at ages T and T+17 for offsets 0 through L−17. Left inversion would extend this agreement to the black left edge at −(T+17) if L−17 ≥ T+11, since the observation is at −6. That cell is white at age T. Hence L ≤ T+27. Applying this to the two possible constant pieces gives at most 3T+81 ticks for the entire coded episode. ## A full-line control

Prescribe the trace at −2 as a concatenation of 0101 and 1010. Both monochrome four-letter words are absent. Every nine-tick window in this trace also prevents 10101 four columns farther west, for **every** starting right half. A nine-tick trace window and the first eight eastern cells determine the five western readings by four left inversions. We checked all four alignments, all eight choices of three blocks, and all 256 eastern prefixes.

One may fix the singleton's literal initial row from −2 eastward, prescribe any finite genuine trace prefix and then append these blocks. Left permutivity uniquely reconstructs the initial western bits. Most such full rows have infinitely many western black cells. In fact an arbitrary initial trace prefix fixes the corresponding finite western prefix, and the rightmost light-cone tip lets the first bit of every other block specify that one more initial western cell is white. The other blocks remain free. As in the preceding strip report, excluding the countably many eventually periodic traces at each site leaves examples with every column aperiodic. These controls explain why enforcing only the right origin or more bounded strip information loses the singleton. Their block density is exactly one half, whereas the west-two trace under a genuine period-three center has limiting density one third or two thirds. They give no period-three center counterexample.

## An exact test for the 011 branch

At a site i let C(n) count the black readings before n and A(n) count the spatial word 10 at sites (i,i+1). Define D(n) = n − 3C(n) + 6A(n). Across any ordinary Rule 30 spacetime, **D is bounded above exactly when column i+2 eventually repeats 011 up to rotation**. Otherwise D tends to positive infinity. For the singleton center, i = −2. This identifies an equality case of the kit's density polygon. It does not require assuming that a limiting density exists. [Existing density region](https://github.com/WolframInstitute/Rule30PrizeKit/blob/bd5ec59a/lean/Ruliology/CellularAutomata/Rule30DensityPolygon.lean)

The proof adds a bounded correction to each tick's charge. On three consecutive cells (q,r,s), let H depend on (r,s), with H(00)=3, H(01)=2, H(10)=0 and H(11)=1. The loss is the tick's charge plus H(next)−H(now). It is always nonnegative. If the three successive eastern readings at t,t+1,t+2 contain exactly two black cells, the loss at t is zero. Conversely, zero losses at t and t+1 force exactly two black cells in that triple. On a tail, overlapping triples with weight two give precisely the three rotations of 011. Summing the losses telescopes. This proves both the boundedness equivalence and the divergence alternative.

Condrey's existing estimate gives D(n) ≥ A(n)−3. Thus infinitely many spatial 10 readings at (−2,−1) would suffice to exclude 011. If A is bounded, the question becomes whether n−3C(n) escapes every upper bound. The coefficient six distinguishes two corners: the kit realizes full histories with C(n)=3n/4+O(1) and A(n)=n/4+O(1). On those histories the coefficient-five expression is bounded while the coefficient-six expression diverges. [Density bound](https://github.com/WolframInstitute/Rule30PrizeKit/blob/bd5ec59a/lean/Ruliology/CellularAutomata/Rule30SourceObservables.lean) [Corner histories](https://github.com/WolframInstitute/Rule30PrizeKit/blob/bd5ec59a/lean/Ruliology/CellularAutomata/Rule30DensityCornerHistories.lean)

For the actual singleton, **at least one of the bonds (−2,−1) and (−1,0) incurs a positive loss during every interval [U,2U+5]**. Otherwise the center and its eastern neighbor would have equal windows at U and U+3 long enough for Jen's left-edge contradiction. This does not force the loss onto the western bond. The period-three orbit on a ring of 12 with successive row masks 3984, 249, 2319 gives a zero three-tick deficit on the western bond and a deficit of three on the next one. It extends to an infinite periodic initial row. Truncating outside a finite light cone reproduces any prescribed finite duration with a finite seed. This rules out a uniform local transfer of the neighboring loss.

The kit verifier kernel-replayed the boundedness and divergence argument, its specialization to the singleton, the count identity, and the singleton deadline. The joint marker recurrence and the divergence at the **particular** western bond remain open. The new equivalence addresses 011; the 001 branch remains separate.

There is a sharper reason the adjacent-bond deadline stalls under the actual 011 hypothesis. In an aligned cycle the center reads (0,1,1) and column −1 reads (a,0,1). Thus the increment of D on (−1,0) is exactly 3a. Here a is the complement of the right gate at the white phase. If the center of the singleton followed 011, an eventually constant gate would contradict Jen, and the kit's finite-right entry and gap theorems bound the gap between successive late a=1 samples by 17 cycles. Consequently the neighboring bond would already accumulate charge at least linearly. The doubling-window deadline by itself cannot force any of that charge onto (−2,−1). The gap application is a paper deduction using the kit's existing theorem. ## Attempted finite-west descent

For the 011 drive, try a quantity P on the finite western row that strictly decreases every successful three ticks. Allow an arbitrary function of the five cells nearest the center and arbitrary real weights for all 31 nonzero five-cell spatial words, summed over all western starting positions. The all-white word has weight zero so the sum is finite. The coefficients can have either sign.

The exact certificate in `mature-k5.json` defeats every strict three-tick descent of this form, including at an interior step of a genuine 27-update 011 episode. There are 19 finite full rows with positive integer multiplicities. On every one, forward evolution verifies the center clock. Across their designated three-tick steps the weighted sum of the changes of **each** of the 63 features is exactly zero. A strict decrease on every row would give a negative sum. The finite right prefixes come from the 011-driven right evolution started empty and warmed for 900 or more ticks. Left inversion supplies a western prefix for 27 further ticks; the deeper western tail in each certificate entry is finite. The certified step begins at tick 12 of that full-row episode. These seeds need not be singleton descendants. Longer histories, other observables and singleton-specific descents are outside this veto.
